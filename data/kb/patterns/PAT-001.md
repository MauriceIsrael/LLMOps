# PAT-001 — Supervised closed loop

## Problem

An alarm indicates a condition with a known remediation, but automatic execution is unacceptable because a wrong action during an incident compounds the outage.

## Forces

- Remediation must be fast enough to matter.
- The action must be auditable and attributable.
- Operators are not developers and will not read code at three in the morning.
- Nobody will trust automation they have never seen behave.

## Solution

Alarm to rule engine, which deduplicates, checks the global switch and the per-asset lock, opens the incident, then launches a workflow whose first node is an approval carrying full context: alarm, asset, proposed action, freshness of the plan, latest compliance status. On approval, pre-checks, unitary action, post-checks, incident update. On any failure, stop, restore, escalate.

## Trade-offs

Adds human latency to remediation, which is the point. Requires the approval surface to be usable on a phone at night. Produces a complete audit trail as a by-product.

## When not to use this

Where the action is genuinely reversible, high frequency and low blast radius — restarting a stateless pod, for instance. There, supervision is friction without value; use it only until the behaviour is characterized, then graduate the class under P-004.

## Seen in

One mission-critical mobile network engagement (2026), across transport, container platform and vendor-managed core.
