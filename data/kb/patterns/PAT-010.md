# PAT-010 — Four-plane network separation (Service / Control / Management / OOB)

## Problem

A mission-critical platform that mixes user traffic, signalling, management, and break-glass operations on shared infrastructure creates compound failure modes: a DDoS on the service plane takes down the management plane; a misconfiguration pushed via the management plane can crash the service plane it is supposed to protect; a compromised management path gives an attacker visibility into live session state.

## Forces

- Regulatory frameworks (NIS2, SecNumCloud) require separation between operational and administrative planes.
- `P-009` (Independent Failure Domain): recovery and observability chains must share zero infrastructure with the systems they manage.
- Break-glass access must be available precisely when the main management path has failed.
- Network segmentation must be enforceable by infrastructure, not solely by policy.

## Solution

Enforce **four hermetically separated planes** across the platform:

| Plane | Function | Isolation Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Service** | User-facing application traffic (MCX, GIS, user data) | No management interface reachable from service addresses |
| **Control** | Signalling, session control, mobility, policy enforcement | Rate-limited; separated from user data at L3 minimum |
| **Management** | Configuration, provisioning, FCAPS, ITSM, automation southbound | Accessible only from the dedicated management network; PAM-gated |
| **Out-of-Band (OOB)** | Break-glass access, recovery tools, bastion hosts | Physically or cryptographically isolated; independent power and transport path |

Enforcement is architectural:
- VLAN separation is the minimum; physical separation for OOB and Management where threat model requires it.
- The automation engine's southbound path runs in the Management plane; its configuration state lives in Git (Service plane never touches the configuration store directly).
- The OOB bastion has no route to the Service plane; it can only reach management interfaces of infrastructure elements.

## Trade-offs

Increases network complexity and requires disciplined IPAM. Management-plane incidents (e.g. BGP misconfiguration on the management VRF) do not affect user traffic, but they do blind the NOC — operational runbooks must account for operating with reduced visibility.

## When not to use this

Small deployments where physical plane separation is impractical may implement logical separation (VRF + ACL) with a documented risk acceptance. Full four-plane physical separation is required for national PPDR systems and systems seeking SecNumCloud or equivalent qualification.

## Seen in

National mission-critical mobile networks (multi-site, 2025–2026). 3GPP architecture specifications implicitly assume this separation; ETSI NFV and O-RAN explicitly require it for operator plane isolation.
