# PAT-022 — Risk-driven proportionate security baseline

## Problem

Ad-hoc security controls either over-engineer low-risk components or leave critical network and information systems exposed without traceability to actual threats, entity exposure, or service-impact containment.

## Forces

- Measures must be appropriate and proportionate to the entity's exposure, size, and the likelihood and severity of incidents.
- Accreditation and audit require every security control in the architecture to trace back to a documented risk analysis and recognised standards (such as ISO/IEC 27001).
- Incident impact must be bounded so a failure in one service does not cascade to recipients or neighbouring services.

## Solution

Anchor the architecture in a documented risk analysis covering all network and information systems in scope, mapping each technical, operational, and organisational measure to the risks it treats and justifying its level against the state of the art and applicable European or international standards. Explicitly specify containment boundaries (isolation, degraded modes, blast-radius limits) to prevent or minimise incident impact on service recipients and adjacent services.

## Trade-offs

Requires maintaining a living risk-to-measure traceability matrix across architecture revisions, preventing unmotivated security exemptions or blind copy-pasting of controls.

## When not to use this

Never omit risk proportionality on production or regulated systems; only disposable local developer sandboxes with zero external exposure may use a minimal baseline.
