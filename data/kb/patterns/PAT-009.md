# PAT-009 — Dual-mode EPC/5GC progressive core migration

## Problem

A mission-critical mobile network must deliver MCX services continuously while migrating from a 4G EPC core to a 5G Standalone (SA) architecture. A hard cutover is operationally unacceptable: a failed migration at 02:00 means emergency calls fail.

## Forces

- MCX service continuity is non-negotiable during migration phases.
- Not all radio sites and devices will be 5G SA-capable simultaneously.
- The vendor's upgrade schedule and the programme's contractual delivery dates may not align.
- No migration test in a lab fully captures a live production system under load.

## Solution

Deploy a **dual-mode EPC/5GC core** from day one. Both EPC and 5GC NFs run concurrently in the same platform. MCX services attach through the mode appropriate to the device's RAN capability. The 5GC is activated progressively: first for new RAN sites, then for migrated sites, finally decommissioning the EPC only after a shadow validation period (`PAT-002`) demonstrates zero session loss.

Migration gates are enforced architecturally, not by policy alone:
- A device that registers on EPC cannot accidentally trigger a 5GC path.
- Rollback is a configuration change in the automation chain, not a physical operation.
- The MCX application layer is core-mode agnostic; it sees services, not NAS stacks.

## Trade-offs

Increases platform footprint and operational complexity during the migration window. Requires the compute platform to support both EPC and 5GC NFs simultaneously. Demands rigorous CMDB tracking of per-site core mode to avoid dual-registration anomalies.

## When not to use this

Where the programme timeline allows a greenfield deployment with no legacy EPC to migrate from — in that case, deploy 5G SA directly and avoid the dual-mode overhead.

## Seen in

National MCX systems (multi-lot programmes, 2025–2026) where contractual MCX service availability is committed before all RAN sites are 5G SA-ready.
