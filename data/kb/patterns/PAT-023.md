# PAT-023 — Supplier vulnerability assessment and SBOM-backed supply chain governance

## Problem

Modern platforms depend on third-party hardware, software, cloud, and managed service suppliers whose specific vulnerabilities, development practices, and upstream dependencies can compromise the entire system if not assessed and contractually governed.

## Forces

- Generic vendor questionnaires miss component-level vulnerabilities and EU coordinated supply-chain risk assessments (NIS2 Article 22(1)).
- Without contractual obligations for vulnerability disclosure, support-period security updates, and SBOMs, operators cannot react to upstream CVEs in time.

## Solution

Maintain a supplier and service-provider register for every direct hardware, software, cloud, and managed-service dependency in the architecture, recording supplier-specific vulnerability assessments, product quality, and secure development practices. Factor EU coordinated security risk assessments of critical supply chains into component selection, and mandate contractual clauses requiring timely vulnerability notification, security updates throughout the support period, and a machine-readable Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) for software deliverables.

## Trade-offs

Increases procurement and onboarding effort for third-party components and may disqualify vendors that refuse SBOM or vulnerability-notification commitments, but eliminates blind spots in the supply chain.

## When not to use this

For internal throwaway prototypes that are never connected to production networks and contain no third-party code shipped to customers.

## Seen in

NIS2 Article 21(3) supply-chain due diligence in regulated telecom and cloud engagements (2026).
