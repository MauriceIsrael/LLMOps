# PAT-021 — Significant incident qualification, reporting and recipient communication

## Problem

Monitoring chains detect technical anomalies, but regulatory compliance requires deterministic severity thresholds to qualify whether an incident is significant, tamper-evident evidence collection, out-of-band communication to affected service recipients, and structured reporting to the CSIRT or competent authority within strict statutory deadlines (24 h early warning, 72 h notification, 1-month final report).

## Forces

- Statutory clocks (24 h / 72 h / 1 month) start as soon as the entity becomes aware of a significant incident.
- Significance depends on service-level measurements (availability, affected user count, duration, cross-border impact) rather than raw infrastructure alerts alone.
- Customer and recipient communication channels must remain available even when primary production services are degraded.

## Solution

Implement per-service significance thresholds in the centralised monitoring and incident-qualification pipeline using live service measurements (availability, affected users, duration). Route qualified incidents through a tamper-evident evidence retention store and a pre-defined notification workflow with designated roles and contact points for the national CSIRT or competent authority, supporting the 24-hour early warning, 72-hour incident notification (with indicators of compromise), intermediate updates, and 1-month final report (root cause, mitigations, cross-border impact). Maintain an out-of-band communication channel tied to the service catalogue to inform affected service recipients of significant cyber threats and actionable remedies.

## Trade-offs

Requires maintaining per-service impact thresholds, tamper-evident log retention, and crisis-resilient contact lists, in exchange for predictable regulatory reporting under incident pressure.

## When not to use this

In isolated development or lab environments that do not deliver services to external recipients and are outside the scope of statutory incident-notification obligations.
