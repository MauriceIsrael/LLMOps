# PAT-020 — Cybersecurity governance, management-body oversight and training

## Problem

Technical security controls fail in accreditation and operation when management bodies have no formal mechanism to approve the security baseline, accept residual risks with traceability, oversee compliance indicators, or maintain cybersecurity competence across leadership and operating staff.

## Forces

- Management bodies are legally accountable for approving risk-management measures and overseeing their implementation.
- Architecture exceptions and residual risk acceptances often remain informal in slide decks or emails instead of an auditable register.
- Operating staff and leadership need recurring training tied to the actual runbooks and security procedures of the platform.

## Solution

Establish a formal cybersecurity governance register alongside the architecture: list the security baseline, residual risks, and accepted exceptions for explicit approval by the management body, require named management-level sign-off on any architecture decision that deviates from Article 21(2) measures, expose baseline coverage, open findings, and incident trends as oversight indicators, and pair the run organisation with a recurring cybersecurity training plan for management-body members and operating staff backed by up-to-date runbooks and security procedures.

## Trade-offs

Adds governance cadence and sign-off latency to risk exceptions and baseline updates, but makes accountability explicit and auditable without blocking day-to-day operational changes that stay within the approved baseline.

## When not to use this

For ephemeral sandboxes or non-regulated proof-of-concept environments with no production data, no regulated service obligations, and an explicit decommissioning date.

## Seen in

NIS2 Article 20 governance alignment across regulated critical-infrastructure engagements (2026).
