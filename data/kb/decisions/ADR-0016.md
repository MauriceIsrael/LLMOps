# ADR-0016 — HSM mandatory for 5G NAS credentials and MCX key material

## Status

**Active** — applies to all mission-critical mobile network engagements at national scale.

## Context

A national mission-critical mobile network handles long-term subscriber credentials (SUPI/SUCI), MCX group and individual key material (KMS, 3GPP TS 33.179), and PKI root CA private keys. These assets are the trust anchors of the entire system: compromise of any one of them enables impersonation of subscribers, decryption of emergency communications, or forgery of certificates.

Software-only key stores (file-based, database-backed, or TPM-only) are insufficient for this threat model because:
- A compromised container runtime or hypervisor can exfiltrate software-resident keys.
- A rogue administrator with access to the key store host can extract key material without triggering audit events visible to the SOC.
- Regulatory frameworks (NIS2, SecNumCloud, 3GPP TS 33.501 §6.1) either mandate or strongly recommend hardware-backed key operations for nationally critical systems.

## Decision

**Hardware Security Modules (HSM), certified at Common Criteria EAL4+ or equivalent, are mandatory for the following operations in any national mission-critical mobile network:**

| HSM Use Case | Protected Operation | Normative Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 5G NAS — SUPI/SUCI protection | Long-term subscriber key (K), SUPI concealment, AUSF key derivation | 3GPP TS 33.501 §6.1 |
| 5G NAS — Authentication | AUSF/UDM authentication procedures | 3GPP TS 33.501 |
| PKI root CA | Root and offline issuing CA private key generation, storage, signing | Industry best practice, SecNumCloud |
| MCX KMS | Group identity key (GIK), client key material signing (3GPP TS 33.179) | 3GPP TS 33.179 §7 |
| Secrets vault master key | Unsealing key for the secrets management system | Defense-in-depth |

No software-only implementation of any of the above is acceptable for a system at national scale. A documented exception with formal risk acceptance by the client CISO and programme security authority is required before any deviation.

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Cryptographic operations on subscriber credentials are hardware-bound; key material never appears in plaintext outside the HSM boundary.
- A compromised orchestration layer, hypervisor, or administrator account cannot exfiltrate long-term key material.
- HSM audit logs provide tamper-evident evidence for security investigations (`ADR-0008`).
- Compliance with 3GPP TS 33.501, NIS2 Art. 21(2)(h), and SecNumCloud requirements is structurally guaranteed.

**Negative:**
- HSMs introduce a latency overhead for key operations (typically 1–5 ms per operation) — this is acceptable for authentication flows but must be factored into the MCPTT access time budget (`PAT-011`).
- HSM provisioning, clustering, and backup/restore procedures add operational complexity. The break-glass procedure for HSM failure must be defined before go-live.
- CC EAL4+ certified HSMs from approved vendors (e.g., Thales Luna, Utimaco, Entrust) have lead times of 8–16 weeks — procurement must begin at programme start.

## Compliance

| Framework | Article / Clause | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 3GPP TS 33.501 | §6.1, §6.2 | Long-term credentials shall be protected with at least USIM-level security; operator AUSF/UDM key protection is recommended to be hardware-backed |
| NIS2 Directive | Art. 21(2)(h) | Cryptography and encryption measures commensurate with risk |
| SecNumCloud | REQ-CRYPTO-03 | HSM required for root CA and subscriber credential operations in qualified cloud services |
| ISO/IEC 27001 | A.8.24 | Use of cryptography — key management lifecycle including hardware protection |
